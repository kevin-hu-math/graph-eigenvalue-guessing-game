    Round settings

    These settings take effect when you start a new graph.

    Start at 1,000 points. Every valid numeric guess (including a correct one) costs points; hints cost extra. The score stops at zero, but you may keep guessing. Giving up reveals the full spectrum and scores zero.

    Make it your own

    Change the view any time, without starting a new round.

    Colors use a symmetric scale from −max |vᵢ| to +max |vᵢ|. Geometric layouts show the first two coordinates (one-dimensional graphs use a line). Spectral layout is based on the graph Laplacian.

    Find the hidden spectrum

    1. Choose a graph model, number of vertices, and tolerance. Press New graph to apply your settings.
    2. Guess any eigenvalue of the graph’s unweighted adjacency matrix. A guess x finds the nearest remaining eigenvalue in [x − ε, x + ε]. One guess finds one occurrence.
    3. Every numeric guess costs points, whether it succeeds or misses. There is no limit on tries. Repeated eigenvalues need repeated guesses.
    4. Click a found eigenvalue to color the graph by its eigenvector. A hint reveals a unit eigenvector of an unfound eigenvalue, keeping its eigenvalue hidden.
    5. Find all N eigenvalues to finish, or give up to reveal them. Save completed rounds to the leaderboard on this browser.

    Guess closeness: optionally turn on the checkbox beside the guess controls. Green means within ε of the nearest eigenvalue remaining at the time of your guess; the scale fades to red at 10ε or more. It gives no higher/lower direction. You can toggle it any time at no extra point cost. Rounds that use this aid are marked on the local leaderboard.

    Changing the layout, colors, or renderer is free. Round parameters and penalties stay fixed until the next graph. The last 100 guesses are shown.

    Planar model: optional uniform labeled spanning tree, then randomly ordered edges accepted only if planarity is preserved, up to the chosen edge count. This distribution is not uniform over planar graphs.

    Hints: the sign of an eigenvector is arbitrary. For a repeated eigenvalue, the displayed vector is one vector from an orthonormal basis of its eigenspace.
